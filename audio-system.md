# Audio system

Read this reference whenever the source includes speech, music, effects, ambience, or a mixed track.

## Listen before labeling

Listen to the entire source once for overall rhythm, then revisit each suspected cue in context. Record:

- speech cadence and pauses;
- speaker changes and overlap;
- music entry, exit, intensity, and section changes;
- audible SFX and their relationship to visual changes;
- ambience or room tone that affects the cut;
- ducking, masking, clipping, or loudness changes.

## Evidence rules

An onset or waveform peak is evidence of a change in the mixed signal, not proof of an isolated sound effect. Mark a cue `unverified` when speech, music, ambience, and SFX cannot be separated. Use `audio.cue: null` for a candidate card when the cue is not independently confirmed.

Voice identity and music ownership are separate from production analysis. Do not recommend cloning a creator's voice or reusing protected music merely because it is present in the reference. The adaptation plan should specify an authorized replacement or leave the audio open.

## Useful audio relationships

Describe relationships such as:

- a hit or whoosh coincides with a card entry;
- a music change marks a section boundary;
- silence creates space before a reveal;
- captions follow speech while music remains continuous;
- a speaker turn changes the active visual focus.

Use `observed` for what can be heard, `measured` for timestamped changes, and `interpreted` for the proposed function. Do not turn a production guess into a source fact.
