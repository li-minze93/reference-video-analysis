# Reference understanding

Use this reference when reading the whole piece and then returning to representative details. The goal is a production explanation, not a shot list copied from the source.

## Read at three scales

### Whole piece

Establish:

- the viewer's likely question, promise, tension, or payoff;
- the opening hook and the first meaningful change;
- the argument, story, or demonstration as a sequence of beats;
- what persists across the piece and what changes;
- the closing action, conclusion, or CTA;
- the dominant presentation mode: presenter-led, narration-led, dialogue, screen demonstration, graphic explanation, or hybrid.

### Segment and shot

For each meaningful segment, record:

- start/end range and evidence source;
- spoken or displayed content;
- visual state before and after the change;
- the primary focus and supporting layers;
- the change type: cut, reveal, replace, reframe, pan, zoom, stack, highlight, or continuous loop;
- what the change contributes: comprehension, proof, hierarchy, emotion, pace, or transition.

### Representative frame and sound detail

Inspect the first stable state, the change itself, and the settled state. For motion, inspect enough frames to distinguish a real movement from a cut or a capture artifact. For audio, listen to the full section and compare any suspected cue with the speech and music.

## Causal explanation

For each major change, answer:

```text
What changed?
When did it change?
What content or event does it follow?
Why is that change useful to the viewer?
What remains on screen or in the mix afterward?
```

Separate these statements:

- Observation: "A four-card row enters from below in left-to-right order."
- Measurement: "The first card starts at 00:10.55 and the next cards are about 0.50 s apart."
- Interpretation: "The stagger makes the process read as a sequence rather than a static list."
- Unknown: "The source audio does not establish whether each card has a separate click."

## Reusable versus source-specific

Reusable behavior includes relationships such as:

- a heading precedes a row of cards;
- a highlighted center item remains readable while a conveyor moves;
- a visual enters at a named phrase and holds through a semantic range;
- a short white flash separates two information pages.

Source-specific content includes:

- the original creator's face, voice, logo, claims, screenshots, product names, and exact wording;
- an image or video asset that cannot be replaced without changing its identity;
- a one-off joke, fact, or transition motivated only by the source narrative.

The analysis package must label this distinction so the adaptation plan can retain a mechanism while replacing the content.
